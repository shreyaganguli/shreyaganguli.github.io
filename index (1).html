About Me

I'm a mechanical engineering student driven by curiosity about how engineered systems interact with the physical world — whether that's a wing cutting through air or a device interfacing with the human body.

NASA JSC Biomedical
3D-Printed Forearm Model for Arm Offloading Research

Developed a patient-realistic forearm model at NASA Johnson Space Center to support arm offloading device testing. Evaluated iPhone and Android 3D scanning apps, processed scan data in Creo Parametric, and redesigned the model to include an internal 1"×1" aluminum channel, a precision IMU mounting surface with caliper-measured bolt holes, and through-hole fasteners. Printed in ABS on a Raise3D Pro2 with custom settings to prevent warping — significantly improving IMU stability and sensor accuracy for offloading algorithm development.

UConn Engineering Biomedical
Automated Knee Exoskeleton Brace

Designed and built a functional knee exoskeleton brace during UConn's Engineering summer program. Starting from hand-sketched dimensioned drawings and cardboard prototypes, the final brace integrates an Arduino microcontroller, a load cell for force sensing, and an ultrasonic distance sensor, programmed in C++ and Python to automate assistive actuation based on real-time sensor feedback. Covered the full engineering design cycle — ideation, prototyping, fabrication, electronics integration, and software control.

ME302 Mechatronics
Turtle-Themed Combat Robot — BattleBot

Designed and built a turtle-themed combat robot for ME302 as part of a team competition. Contributed SolidWorks CAD modeling of a custom wheel (Ø55mm, angled tread pattern, press-fit axle hub) and elliptical shell/armor (215×316mm, 1:4 scale drawing). Created a full assembly with a 12-part BOM including motors, ESCs, receiver, battery, and 3D-printed mounts, and applied soldering for all motor and ESC wiring. The project involved multiple design iterations — wheels were resized twice to balance ground clearance and motor fit, and baseplate component spacing was revised to accommodate wiring. A key takeaway was discovering that 3D-printed parts deviate slightly from CAD dimensions, requiring tolerance adjustments in the final design.

Biomedical Assistive Tech
Arthritis Door-Opening Aid

Designed a portable ergonomic assistive device for rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis patients who struggle to grip and twist keys or doorknobs. Backed by 7 peer-reviewed sources, the dual-function device holds a key via friction fit on one end, and uses interlocking C-shaped TPU arms on the other that automatically close around a doorknob when pressed — converting a simple push into a firm, pain-free grip. The PLA chassis provides rigidity while TPU arms provide compliance and traction. Developed through structured HMW ideation, literature research, sketching, and multi-material 3D prototyping.
